# Global Liquidity Regimes & US Equity Multiples

In equity market valuation, narrative follows price, but price invariably follows **systemic dollar liquidity**. 

While equity research frequently focuses on quarterly EPS beats, bottom-up analyst estimates often fail during regime shifts because they treat the cost of capital as an exogenous, static variable. In institutional portfolio management, aggregate equity multiple expansion is primarily driven by the **net availability of global high-powered money**.

This memorandum deconstructs the mechanics of **Fed Net Liquidity**, analyzes the terminal state of the **Overnight Reverse Repo Facility (ON RRP)**, and models the compression dynamics of the **US Equity Risk Premium (ERP)**.

---

## 1. The Net Liquidity Equation & The S&P 500

Headline Federal Reserve balance sheet size (WALCL) is an incomplete measure of market liquidity. To understand the actual liquid reserves available to commercial banks and primary dealers, one must track **Federal Reserve Net Liquidity**:

$$\text{Net Liquidity} = \text{Fed Total Assets} - (\text{Treasury General Account (TGA)}) - (\text{Overnight Reverse Repo (ON RRP)})$$

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     FEDERAL RESERVE ASSET BALANCE                        |
|                               (WALCL)                                    |
+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
                                     |
              +----------------------+----------------------+
              |                                             |
              v                                             v
     [ LIQUIDITY ABSORBERS ]                       [ USABLE RESERVES ]
     1. Treasury General Account (TGA)            Commercial Bank Deposits
     2. Overnight Reverse Repo (ON RRP)            Available for Securities Lending
              |                                             |
              +----------------------+----------------------+
                                     |
                                     v
                  +--------------------------------------+
                  |   NET LIQUIDITY EXPANSION / DRAIN    |
                  |     (Direct Correlation to S&P P/E)  |
                  +--------------------------------------+
```

### Transmission Mechanics:
1. **TGA Fluctuations**: When the US Treasury issues debt and builds cash balances at the Fed (TGA expansion), liquidity is drained from the commercial banking sector. Conversely, quarterly tax disbursements and debt ceiling drawdowns inject cash directly into primary dealer accounts.
2. **Reverse Repo Facility (RRP)**: During the 2022–2024 Quantitative Tightening (QT) cycle, the rapid drain of the RRP from \$2.5 trillion down to negligible levels acted as a powerful synthetic quantitative easing, completely neutralizing the headline reduction in Fed securities holdings.

## 3. Equity Risk Premium (ERP) Compression

The **Equity Risk Premium (ERP)** represents the excess earnings yield of the equity market over the risk-free benchmark:

$$\text{ERP} = \text{Forward Earnings Yield} - \text{10Y US TIPS Real Yield}$$

$$\text{ERP} = \left(\frac{1}{\text{Forward P/E}}\right) - y_{\text{real}}$$

As of late 2026, with the 10-year US Treasury real yield anchored near 2.10% and the S&P 500 trading at 21.5x forward earnings (an earnings yield of 4.65%), the implied ERP sits below **255 basis points**—its tightest spread in over two decades.

```
Historical ERP Spread Distribution (1990 - 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
2000 Dot-Com Peak:           -120 bps   [ Extreme Complacency ]
2008 GFC Peak Stress:        +780 bps   [ Peak Panic / Generational Buy ]
2015-2019 Average:           +420 bps   [ Normal Equilibrium ]
Current 2026 Level:          +255 bps   [ Low Margin of Safety ]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
```

Historically, an ERP compressed below 300 bps leaves zero room for negative macro shocks or execution slips in hyperscaler CapEx. When capital carries a real cost, valuations must be defended by superior, compounding Free Cash Flow (FCF) rather than multiple expansion.

## 4. Hyperscaler CapEx vs Free Cash Flow Conversion

The modern equity index is dominated by mega-cap technology hyperscalers. Over the past 36 months, aggregate CapEx for the top five platforms expanded by over 68%, driven primarily by accelerated computing infrastructure.

```
Hyperscaler Capital Allocation Shift:
[Operating Cash Flow] ---> High AI Infrastructure CapEx (Datacenters/Silicon)
                      ---> Depressed Short-Term Free Cash Flow Conversion
                      ---> Elevated Hurdle Rate Requirement (WACC > 9.5%)
```

If enterprise monetisation of AI infrastructure follows an extended S-curve rather than an immediate linear ramp, return on invested capital (ROIC) will temporarily compress below the weighted average cost of capital (WACC), triggering a de-rating across non-hardware beneficiaries.

## 5. Strategic Conclusion & Portfolio Implications

1. **Multiples Are Range-Bound**: With the RRP buffer exhausted, net liquidity will not support S&P 500 multiple expansion beyond 22x in the absence of explicit central bank balance sheet expansion.
2. **Prioritize Real FCF Yield**: Investors should underweight high-multiple companies reliant on terminal value discounting and overweight balance sheets with self-funding cash generation, low leverage, and pricing power.
3. **Monitor Treasury Issuance Mix**: Any shift in Treasury refunding toward shorter-duration bills (T-bills) will temporarily alleviate commercial bank reserve pressures, creating tactical liquidity windows.
